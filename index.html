1. Problem of interest

1.1 Context

The thyroid gland is a butterfly-shaped endocrine gland that releases hormones to control the body’s metabolism, energy and growth. The removal of the thyroid gland (thyroidectomy) is a surgical intervention offered to patients with thyroid cancers, goiters, Graves disease and thyroid nodules, with thyroid cancer accounting for the bulk of cases due to its high prevalence. Thyroid cancer is the second most common cancer in females between 25 and 49 years old (NCCS, 2026), and almost 95% of cases require surgery as an effective treatment option (Gomez-Saiz, 2013). The recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN) is an important nerve that controls voice, breathing and swallowing functions through activation of the vocalis muscle, and is a key nerve at risk during thyroidectomies due its close positioning posterior to the thyroid with an injury rate of about 8.2% in Singapore (Soong et al., 2026). The presence of large tumours in thyroid cancer can obstruct the surgeon’s field view of the RLN, increasing the risk of injury.

RLN injuries can be temporary or permanent, with a permanent injury rate of 1%, and can either be unilateral or bilateral. While unilateral RLN injury can cause swallowing difficulties and voice changes (Culp & Patel, 2023), bilateral injury can cause serious complications like respiratory disease (Culp & Patel, 2023). RLN injury can be classified into two subtypes: type I (segmental injury) or type II (global injury). Type I injuries account for 70% of cases, and are often caused by clamping, traction, compression or thermal injury and have the potential to be corrected intraoperatively if a clip, suture, vessel or fibrotic band entrapping the nerve at this point is removed on time (Wu et al., 2014).

Intra-operative nerve monitoring (IONM) system is during thyroidectomies to guide surgical decision making during thyroidectomies by allowing for RLN identification and determination of functional intactness, for surgeons to preserve the RLN or to change their operative course if they determine the nerve to be damaged. To date, there is only one IONM system that is widely distributed worldwide — Medtronic’s Neural Integrity Monitor (NIM), with the most recent model being the NIM Vital. Since the RLN controls the vocalis muscle, IONM determines RLN functional intactness by determining the RLN’s ability to stimulate the vocalis muscle. This is achieved by 1) electrical stimulation of the RLN and 2) measurement of the action potential fired by the vocalis muscle as a result of RLN stimulation.
