1. Problem of interest

1.1 Context

The thyroid gland is a butterfly-shaped endocrine gland that releases hormones to control the body’s metabolism, energy and growth. The removal of the thyroid gland (thyroidectomy) is a surgical intervention offered to patients with thyroid cancers, goiters, Graves disease and thyroid nodules, with thyroid cancer accounting for the bulk of cases due to its high prevalence. Thyroid cancer is the second most common cancer in females between 25 and 49 years old (NCCS, 2026), and almost 95% of cases require surgery as an effective treatment option (Gomez-Saiz, 2013). The recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN) is an important nerve that controls voice, breathing and swallowing functions through activation of the vocalis muscle, and is a key nerve at risk during thyroidectomies due its close positioning posterior to the thyroid with an injury rate of about 8.2% in Singapore (Soong et al., 2026). The presence of large tumours in thyroid cancer can obstruct the surgeon’s field view of the RLN, increasing the risk of injury.
